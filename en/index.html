01 · Why I built it

The seller picked the reviews on the wall

A course's review wall shows 100 glowing reviews because the seller picked them. The most important rule: if it can't find criticism, it says so, and explains why (too new? the community lives in a private Discord?).

02 · How to use it

Three steps, no sign-up

03 · What it checks

Not just reviews: the instructor too

The official pageWhat you are actually buying

Price, one-off or subscription, refund policy, number of lessons, last update, and whether any lesson is still marked "in production" (you'd be buying futures).

Real voicesAway from the official site

PTT, Dcard, Threads, public Facebook groups, Vocus, blogs, YouTube comments. It searches both positive and negative keywords.

The instructorWhere course quality starts

Two columns: facts that can be cross-checked, and claims only they make. Then one question: do they practise this field, or mainly teach it?

AlternativesThe opportunity cost

Whether free resources are enough. An instructor's free content is usually the floor of the paid course.

04 · Evidence grades

Every review gets a stamp

The most common way AI gets reviews wrong: it finds the seller's "100+ happy students" wall and concludes the course is well loved.

A

Independent long-form reviews

Most trustworthy

B

Scattered forum and social posts

Read the trend, not single posts

C

Posts with discount codes or affiliate links

Treat as ads

D

Testimonials on the seller's site

Not evidence of reputation

05 · Nine red flags

Checked one by one at the end

Each marked ✅ fine, ⚠️ watch out, or 🚩 high risk.

  • Artificial scarcity and countdowns
  • No refund policy
  • Promised income or results
  • Only the seller's own testimonials
  • On sale before it is finished
  • Closed community, no real feedback
  • No verifiable work by the instructor
  • Instructor's record can't be checked
  • Free resources already cover it

06 · Honesty rules

The most important part of the prompt: no criticism found is not the same as a good reputation. No made-up reviews or links, a real URL for every quote, facts kept apart from inference, and if the price isn't published, it says so instead of guessing.

07 · FAQ

Questions

Does it cost anything? Do I need an account?

No and no. Free, no sign-up, open source. Just open it and use it.

Does anyone collect what I type?

The tool is a static page with no backend. What you type stays in your own browser for next time; when you send it, it goes to the AI you picked.

Why do some platforms need me to paste?

Prefill parameters are a convention, not a public API, and Gemini and Meta AI don't have one. So every button copies to your clipboard first; the prefill is a bonus, and nothing breaks if it stops working.

Can I trust the AI's verdict as is?

No. Check the results yourself and open the cited links. That's why the prompt demands a real URL for every quote.

What is the Threads version for?

There are reviews on Threads that outside search engines can't reach, and the honest ones are often in the replies. The Threads version, used with Meta AI, only collects them without a verdict; paste the list back into the full report as grade B evidence.

Can I modify it?

Use it however you like, and share your changes too. The source is on GitHub.
